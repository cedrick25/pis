# 1. How to use this manual

## 1.1 What PPIS is (in simple terms)

PPIS (**Parole and Probation Information System**) is a **website** your office uses to store and work with case-related information on a computer. You do **not** install special software on your PC to use it—you use a normal web browser, like visiting any other secure office website.

## 1.2 Symbols used in this guide

- **NOTE:** Extra information that helps avoid mistakes.  
- **Step 1, Step 2, …** Follow the steps in order the first time.  
- **Figure** A place where you should insert a screenshot later.

---

# 2. What you need before you start

## 2.1 Computer and internet

**Purpose:** Make sure PPIS can load and respond normally.

**Step 1:** Use an office computer or another approved device.

**Step 2:** Connect to the internet or your office network the way your IT unit instructs (for example, office Wi‑Fi or VPN).

**Expected result:** You can open other trusted websites without errors.

**NOTE:** If your office requires VPN or a fixed network, PPIS may not open from a public network.

## 2.2 Account details from your administrator

**Purpose:** You need personal login details that only you should use.

**Step 1:** Ask your **PPIS administrator** or **IT help desk** for:

- The **official web address (URL)** for PPIS, and  
- Your **username** (often an email address), and  
- Your **first password** (you may be asked to change it later).

**Expected result:** You have the address written down or saved in a **secure** place, and you know your username.

**NOTE:** Do not share your password with coworkers, family, or clients.

---

# 3. How to access the website

## 3.1 Purpose

To open PPIS, you must use the **exact** address given to you. Typing the wrong address can send you to the wrong site or show an error.

## 3.2 Step-by-step instructions

**Step 1:** Start your web browser (for example, Microsoft Edge, Google Chrome, or Mozilla Firefox).

**Step 2:** Click the **address bar** at the top of the browser window.

**Step 3:** Type the **full PPIS address** your administrator gave you.

**Step 4:** Press the **Enter** key on your keyboard.

## 3.3 Expected results

- The PPIS **login** (sign-in) page should load.  
- You may see your agency logo or the name **PPIS** on the page.

**Figure 2 — PPIS Login Page**

*[Placeholder: Insert screenshot of the login page.]*

## 3.4 Notes

**NOTE:** If the page does not load, check spelling and spaces in the address. If it still fails, contact IT—your network may block the site or the server may be down for maintenance.

**NOTE:** For faster access later, your IT unit may allow you to **bookmark** the address only on **secure, staff-only** computers.

# 4. How to log in

## 4.1 Purpose

Logging in tells PPIS **who you are** and which menus you are allowed to use. Until you log in, you cannot open case data.

## 4.2 Step-by-step instructions

**Step 1:** On the PPIS login page, find the box for **email address** (or **username**—your screen label may vary).

**Step 2:** Click inside the box. Type your **full** email or username **carefully**. Do not add a space before or after it.

**Step 3:** Click the **Password** box. Type your password. The letters will usually appear as **dots** or **asterisks**—this is normal.

**Step 4:** Click **Sign in** (or the button with the same meaning on your screen).

**Step 5:** Wait a few seconds for the system to respond.

### If your office uses a second step (OTP / one-time code)

**Step 6:** If a new screen asks for a **one-time code**, check your **mobile phone SMS** or **office email** (whichever your agency uses).

**Step 7:** Type the code in the box provided. Click **Enter OTP** or the matching button.

**Step 8:** If the code expires, use **Resend OTP** only when the button is available, then enter the new code.

- You may see a short **success** message.

# 5. Dashboard overview

## 5.1 Purpose

The **Dashboard** is your **landing page** after login. It confirms that you entered PPIS and gives you a starting point before you open menus.

## 5.2 What you will see

Exact wording may differ, but you will usually see:

- A **welcome** message for PPIS.  
- Your **name** (or account label) on the **top-right** of the screen.  
- The **main menu** on the **left** (or behind a **menu** button on small screens).

## 5.3 Step-by-step: Confirm you are in the right place

**Step 1:** Look at the **top-left** area of the screen for PPIS branding or the **menu** icon (three horizontal lines on smaller displays).

**Step 2:** Look at the **top-right** for your **name** or **profile** area.

**Expected result:** You recognize the layout as PPIS, not another website.

## 5.4 Notes

**NOTE:** Some users may skip the Dashboard and go straight to a **default list**—this depends on how your office configured PPIS.

# 6. Navigation basics

## 6.1 Purpose

**Navigation** means **moving** from one PPIS page to another using menus, links, and buttons—without using the browser’s Back button as your main tool.

## 6.2 The main menu (left side)

Menus are **grouped** by type of work. Depending on your **permissions**, you may see only **some** of these groups:

| Menu area (examples) | Plain-language meaning |
|----------------------|-------------------------|
| **Dashboard** | Starting / summary area after login |
| **Probation** | Probation investigation and supervision work lists |
| **Parole and Pardon** | Parole / related investigation and supervision lists |
| **Docket Routing** | Sending or moving dockets between tracks your office uses |
| **PDL Routing** | Person Deprived of Liberty routing (when enabled for your role) |
| **Sent** | Items your office has already sent out |
| **Inbox** | Items waiting to be received or processed |
| **Fact Sheet** | Client fact sheet lists (may be split by program track) |
| **Forms** | Agency forms your office stores in the system |
| **My Organization** | Administration tools (most end users rarely need these) |

## 6.3 Step-by-step: Open a menu area

**Step 1:** Look at the **left menu**. If a name has a **small arrow** or looks like a **section heading**, click it.

**Step 2:** Click the **specific** link you need (for example, a list called **Investigation**, **Supervision**, or another name your office uses).

**Expected result:** The **main area** of the screen changes to show a **title**, **buttons**, and sometimes a **table** (grid) of rows.

**Step 3:** If the menu is hidden, click the **menu toggle** (often **top-left**) to show it again.

## 6.4 Top of the screen (header)

**Purpose:** Common controls and your account menu.

**Step 1:** Identify your **name** or **photo** on the **right**.

**Step 2:** Click it only when you need **Logout** or profile-related choices (see Section 7 and Section 11).

## 6.5 Notes

**NOTE:** If you **do not** see a menu item described in training, your **role** may hide it. Speak to your supervisor—this is usually not a computer fault.

**NOTE:** Some screens show **breadcrumbs** (a path like “Dashboard › …”) near the top. You can click part of the path to go **up** one level **when the text is a link**.

# 7. User account and profile

## 7.1 Purpose

Your **account** ties PPIS access to **you**: your name, office, and permissions. The profile area is mainly for **identifying** you and **logging out**.

## 7.2 Viewing your name and picture

**Step 1:** Sign in to PPIS (Section 4).

**Step 2:** Look at the **top-right** corner for your **name** or **avatar** (picture).

**Expected result:** You see the account you expect. If you see the **wrong** name, sign out immediately and tell IT—do not work on cases under the wrong identity.

**Figure 8 — User profile / account menu**

## 7.3 Who can change account details

**Purpose:** Keep official information accurate and secure.

**Step 1:** If your email, office, or access level is wrong, contact your **PPIS administrator** or **HR/IT**—do **not** ask another employee to “share” their login.

**Expected result:** The administrator updates your record. You may need to **log out** and **log in again** to see new permissions.

## 7.4 Notes

**NOTE:** Password resets, unlocks, and new accounts are **administrator** tasks unless your office gives you a self-service link.

# 8. Basic usage of the system

## 8.1 Purpose

“Basic usage” means everyday actions: opening a **list**, reading **rows**, and using **buttons** your role allows (such as **View** or **Update**).

## 8.2 Typical screens you will use

| Screen type | What it is for |
|------------|----------------|
| **List / table** | Shows many records in rows; each row is one case, client, or docket (wording varies). |
| **View / read-only** | Lets you read details without saving changes. |
| **Update / edit** | Lets you change fields your role allows, then **save**. |
| **Upload / attachments** | Lets you attach files when your procedure requires documents. |

## 8.3 Step-by-step: Open a list and pick a record

**Step 1:** Use the **left menu** to open the area your supervisor assigned (for example, a **docket list** or **fact sheet list**).

**Step 2:** Wait until the **loading** finishes. A table should appear (it may be empty if there is no data).

**Step 3:** Move up and down the list using the **scroll bar** or mouse wheel.

**Step 4:** If your screen has **page numbers** or **Next**, use them to see more rows.

**Expected result:** You can locate a row by reading columns such as names, numbers, or dates—exact column titles depend on the list.

**NOTE:** Buttons may be **color-coded**. If a button is **missing**, your role may not allow that action.

# 9. Searching and filtering records

## 9.1 Purpose

**Search** helps you find one record quickly in a **long** list. **Filter** (if available) limits what the list shows by rules such as date, office, or status.

## 9.2 Step-by-step: Search a list

**Step 1:** Open the correct **list** (Section 8).

**Step 2:** Find the **Search** box (or bar). Read any **hint** inside the box—it may say which fields you can search (name, number, and so on).

**Step 3:** Type your **keyword** or **number**. Avoid extra spaces at the start or end.

**Step 4:** Click **Search** or press **Enter** if your screen works that way.

**Expected result:** The table refreshes and shows **only** rows that match (or a “no results” message).

**Figure 10 — Search bar on a list**

*[Placeholder: Insert screenshot highlighting the search field and button.]*

## 9.3 Step-by-step: Clear a search

**Step 1:** Remove the text in the **Search** box.

**Step 2:** Click **Search** again, or click a **clear** (**×**) icon if the screen provides one.

**Expected result:** The full list (or default filtered list) returns.

## 9.4 Notes

**NOTE:** If search returns **too many** rows, add a **more specific** word or number from your case papers.

**NOTE:** If search never finds a record you know exists, confirm you are in the **correct** menu (probation vs. parole vs. another track).

# 10. Viewing and updating records

## 10.1 Purpose

- **View** = read details safely.  
- **Update** = change data and **save** the new information according to office rules.

## 10.2 Step-by-step: View a record

**Step 1:** Open the list (Section 8).

**Step 2:** Locate the row you need (Section 9 if the list is long).

**Step 3:** Click **View** (or a row link with the same meaning).

**Expected result:** A detail screen opens. Fields may look **read-only** (grayed out or not editable).

**Figure 11 — View record screen**

## 10.3 Step-by-step: Update a record

**Step 1:** From the list, click **Update** (or **Edit**) on the correct row.

**Step 2:** Move through the form fields. Change **only** what your procedure allows.

**Step 3:** When finished, click **Save**, **Submit**, or the button your screen shows.

**Step 4:** Read any **message** that appears at the top or bottom (success in **green**, problems in **red** or **yellow**).

**Expected result:** The system stores your changes, or it tells you **exactly** which fields must be corrected.

**Step 1:** Open **View** or **Update** for the record, or click **Attachments** / **Upload** when shown on the list.

**Step 2:** Follow on-screen steps to **choose a file** from your computer.

**Step 3:** Wait until upload finishes. Read any success or error message.

# 11. Logging out

## 11.1 Purpose

**Logout** ends your session on that browser. This protects client data when you step away or when another person uses the computer.

## 11.2 Step-by-step instructions

**Step 1:** Go to the **top-right** of PPIS.

**Step 2:** Click your **name** or **picture**.

**Step 3:** Click **Logout** (or **Sign out**).

**Step 4:** Wait until the **login** page appears (or until PPIS clearly closed your session).

## 11.3 Expected results

- You return to the **login** screen, **or**  
- You see a short message that you signed out.

**Figure 14 — Logout in the profile menu**

## 11.4 Notes

**NOTE:** Closing the browser **without** logging out may be risky on **shared** PCs. Prefer **Logout** first.

# 12. If something goes wrong

**Purpose:** Quick checks before you call support.

| Problem | What to try |
|--------|-------------|
| Blank page or endless loading | Refresh once; check internet; try again later; report to IT if others are affected. |
| “Invalid” login | Retype username/password slowly; check **Caps Lock**; ask for a **password reset** if unsure. |
| Menu items missing | Confirm you used your **own** account; ask your supervisor about **permissions**. |
| Search finds nothing | Clear search; confirm correct **module**; try another keyword from official papers. |
| Cannot save / red errors | Read the field message; fill **required** items; try again after correcting. |

---

# 13. How to get help

**Step 1:** For **access** problems (password, account locked, wrong office), contact your **PPIS administrator** or **IT help desk**.

**Step 2:** For **how to encode** or **what to put in a field**, follow your **unit supervisor’s** office procedures and approved forms.

**Step 3:** For **browser** or **network** errors, note the **exact** message text, **time**, and **screen name** before you call support—this speeds up repairs.
