## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Who this manual is for

This manual is written for **staff and authorized users** who work with **probation investigation dockets** in PPIS. You do **not** need technical or programming knowledge to follow these steps.

**Figure 1 — Sample login page (place your screenshot here)**

---

### 1.2 What PPIS does (in simple terms)

**Purpose:** PPIS (**Parole and Probation Information System**) is a web-based tool that helps your organization store, view, and work with probation and parole-related records in a structured way.

**What you do in practice:** You open PPIS in a web browser, sign in, use the menus to go to the right module, then view or update records you are responsible for.

---

### 1.3 What the Probation Investigation module is for

**Purpose:** The **Probation — Investigation** area is where you work with **investigation dockets** for probation: a central list of dockets with details such as docket number, dates, client name, case information, and field office. From this list you can **view** details, **update** information (if you have permission), or go to **attachments** (files) for a docket.

**Expected result when you use it correctly:** You can quickly find a docket, confirm that the details match your records, and complete the updates or file actions your office requires.

**NOTE:** “Courtesy Investigation” under **Probation** is a **different** submenu item. This pilot manual covers **Investigation** only, not Courtesy Investigation.

## 2. Getting started

### 2.1 How to open the PPIS website

**Purpose:** To start PPIS, you need a device with internet access and a supported web browser (such as Microsoft Edge, Google Chrome, or Mozilla Firefox).

**Step 1.** Open your web browser.

**Expected result:** You see an empty browser window or your usual home page.

**Step 2.** Click the **address bar** at the top of the browser (the long box where website addresses appear).

**Expected result:** The address bar is highlighted and ready for typing.

**Step 3.** Type the **PPIS website address** given to you by your administrator, then press **Enter** on your keyboard.

**Expected result:** The PPIS sign-in page loads.

**NOTE:** If you do not have the exact link, ask your **IT support** or **system administrator**. Do not guess the address.

**Figure 2 — Browser address bar with PPIS URL (place your screenshot here)**

---

### 2.2 How to sign in

**Purpose:** Signing in proves who you are so PPIS can show the correct menus and records for your field office.

**Step 1.** On the sign-in page, find the box labeled for your **email address** (the page may say “Email address”).

**Expected result:** You see an empty text box next to a small user icon.

**Step 2.** Click inside the email box. Type your **authorized email address** carefully. Check for spelling and extra spaces.

**Expected result:** Your email appears in the box.

**Step 3.** Click inside the **password** box. Type your password.

**Expected result:** Dots or bullets appear instead of plain characters. This is normal.

**Step 4.** Click the **Sign in** button.

**Expected result:** The system checks your details. If they are correct, PPIS continues and may show another step (see section 2.3) or take you to the **Dashboard** or your landing page.

**Figure 3 — PPIS sign-in fields and Sign in button (place your screenshot here)**

**NOTE:** If your office uses **Sign Up Here** for new accounts, follow your supervisor’s instructions. Many users will already have an account created for them.

---

### 2.3 If you are asked for a one-time code (OTP)

**Purpose:** Some sign-in flows send a **one-time code** to your registered device or channel for extra security.

**Step 1.** If the page changes and asks for an **OTP**, look at the on-screen timer or instructions.

**Expected result:** You know how long the code is valid and where to find it (SMS, email, or token—according to your office policy).

**Step 2.** Type the code into the **OTP** field.

**Expected result:** The digits or characters appear in the box.

**Step 3.** Click the button to **confirm** the code (for example **Enter OTP**).

**Expected result:** If the code is correct, PPIS signs you in.

**Step 4.** If the code expired, use **Resend OTP** if that button appears and your policy allows it. Wait for the new code and try again.

**Expected result:** You receive a fresh code and can complete sign-in.

**Figure 4 — OTP entry screen (place your screenshot here)**

**NOTE:** If you never receive a code, contact your **IT help desk**. Do not share OTPs with other people.

---

### 2.4 If you cannot sign in

**Step 1.** Read any **red or highlighted message** on the sign-in page.

**Expected result:** The message may say your email or password was wrong, or your account is locked.

**Step 2.** Try again slowly, watching for **Caps Lock** on your keyboard.

**Expected result:** If it was a typing mistake, sign-in may succeed.

**Step 3.** If it still fails, contact your **system administrator** or **IT support**. Say what message you saw (or take a screenshot if allowed).

**Expected result:** Support can reset your password or fix your account settings.

## 6. Probation — Investigation module

### 6.1 Purpose of the Investigation docket list

**Purpose:** This screen lists **investigation dockets** for your **field office** so you can locate a case quickly, confirm key details, and open the docket to **view**, **update**, or manage **attachments**.

**Expected result:** You see a **table** with columns such as row number, **Docket Number**, **Received Date**, **Name**, **Criminal Case No.**, **Field Office**, and **Actions** buttons.

---

### 6.2 Opening the Investigation docket list

**Step 1.** In the left menu, locate the section labeled **Probation**.

**Expected result:** You see submenu items under Probation.

**Step 2.** If **Probation** is collapsed, click **Probation** once to expand it.

**Expected result:** Items such as **Investigation**, **Courtesy Investigation**, **Supervision**, and **Courtesy Supervision** appear.

**Step 3.** Click **Investigation** (not “Courtesy Investigation”).

**Expected result:** The **Investigation Docket List** page opens. The breadcrumbs show **Dashboard › Docketing › Investigation Docket list**, and the card title reads **Investigation Docket List**.

**Figure 11 — Probation menu with Investigation selected (place your screenshot here)**

**Figure 12 — Investigation Docket List page (place your screenshot here)**

**NOTE:** If you do **not** see **Probation** or **Investigation**, your account may lack permission. Ask your administrator.

---

### 6.3 Understanding the docket list table

**Purpose:** Each row is one docket your office can work with in this module.

**Typical columns**

- **#** — Row number on the current page.  
- **Docket Number** — The reference number of the docket.  
- **Received Date** — The date received by the field office (if recorded).  
- **Name** — The client or subject name tied to the docket.  
- **Criminal Case No.** — The criminal case reference, if available.  
- **Field Office** — The office associated with the docket.  
- **Actions** — Buttons such as **View**, **Update**, **Attachments**, and sometimes **Remove**, depending on your access.

**Step 1.** Scroll sideways if the table has many columns and they do not all fit on your screen.

**Expected result:** You can see all columns without guessing.

**Figure 13 — Investigation docket table with columns (place your screenshot here)**

**NOTE:** While the list is loading, you may briefly see **Loading dockets…**. Wait until the table fills in.

---

### 6.4 Searching and filtering dockets

**Purpose:** The search box helps you find a docket when the list is long. You can search by **docket number**, **criminal case (CC) number**, or **name** (as supported by the system).

**Step 1.** Look above the table for a **Search** label, a **text box**, and a **search** button (magnifying glass icon).

**Expected result:** You see the search controls on the right side of the table controls row on larger screens.

**Step 2.** Click in the search box. Type a **docket number**, **case number**, or **name** (or part of it). Avoid extra spaces at the beginning or end.

**Expected result:** Your typing appears in the box.

**Step 3.** Click the **Search** button (with the magnifying glass).

**Expected result:** The table refreshes and shows rows that match your search.

**Step 4.** To clear what you typed, click the **small X** inside or beside the search box (if shown), delete the text manually, then run search again if needed.

**Expected result:** The search field is empty and you can start a new search.

**Figure 14 — Search box and Search button on Investigation list (place your screenshot here)**

**Expected messages**

- If your **field office** cannot be determined, you may see an error asking you to **sign in again**. Follow section 2 and, if the problem continues, contact support.

**NOTE:** An empty search shows the regular paginated list for your office, not “every office in the country.” The system is scoped to your authorized office.

---

### 6.5 Changing how many rows appear on a page

**Purpose:** You can show more or fewer rows at once (for example 10, 25, 50, or 100).

**Step 1.** Look for a **Show [number] entries** (or similar) control near the top-left of the table.

**Expected result:** You see a drop-down with numbers.

**Step 2.** Click the drop-down and choose a larger or smaller page size.

**Expected result:** The table reloads with that many rows per page.

**Figure 15 — Page length control (Show entries) (place your screenshot here)**

### 6.6 Viewing a docket (read-only)

**Purpose:** **View** lets you read docket details without changing them (unless the screen offers separate edit controls).

**Step 1.** Find the row for the docket you need.

**Expected result:** The row shows the correct **Docket Number** and **Name**.

**Step 2.** In the **Actions** column, click **View** (eye icon).

**Expected result:** PPIS opens the **investigation docket view** page for that docket. Fields appear in sections, often as read-only boxes you can scroll through.

**Step 3.** Read the sections you need (subject details, case information, dates, and other data shown by your office’s form).

**Expected result:** You confirm information for reports, phone calls, or on-site work.

**Step 4.** Use **breadcrumbs** or your browser **Back** button only if you are sure it is safe in your environment; otherwise use **Investigation** in the menu to return to the list.

**Expected result:** You are back on the Investigation Docket List.

**Figure 16 — View docket screen (place your screenshot here)**

---

### 6.7 Updating a docket

**Purpose:** **Update** opens the editable investigation form so you can record new or corrected information according to your office procedures.

**Step 1.** On the Investigation Docket List, locate the correct docket row.

**Expected result:** The row is visible on the current page or found through search (section 6.4).

**Step 2.** Click **Update** in the **Actions** column for that row.

**Expected result:** The **update** page opens with form fields and section headers. Some parts may be disabled if they are not meant to be edited.

**Step 3.** Move through each section. Click section headers if they expand or collapse (accordion-style), as designed on the page.

**Expected result:** You can reach all fields you are expected to complete.

**Step 4.** Type or choose values in the fields according to your **standard operating procedures**.

**Expected result:** The form shows your new values.

**Step 5.** Submit or save using the button your form provides (for example **Save** or **Update**—the exact label depends on the current page).

**Expected result:** A success message appears or the system returns you to the list. If an error message appears, read it, correct the field mentioned, and try again.

**Figure 17 — Update docket form (place your screenshot here)**

**NOTE:** Do not use **Update** for changes you are not authorized to make. If a field is unclear, ask your supervisor before entering data.

---

### 6.8 Working with attachments / file upload screen

**Purpose:** The **Attachments** (or file upload) screen lets you see or add files tied to the docket, such as PDFs or scanned documents, according to policy.

**Step 1.** On the Investigation Docket List, click **Attachments** for the chosen docket (if your role shows this button).

**Expected result:** PPIS opens the file upload / attachments page for that docket.

**Step 2.** Follow the on-screen tabs or instructions to **upload** a new file or **view** / **download** existing files as your office allows.

**Expected result:** Files you add appear in the list after a successful upload; existing files open or download correctly.

**Figure 18 — Investigation attachments / file upload screen (place your screenshot here)**

**NOTE:** Only upload documents you are permitted to store in PPIS. Remove or replace files only when policy allows.

---

### 6.9 Removing a docket (when you are allowed to)

**Purpose:** **Remove** permanently deletes a docket from this list. It is for exceptional cases and roles that are explicitly allowed.

**Step 1.** Click **Remove** (or similar) on the row, if you see it.

**Expected result:** A confirmation dialog appears warning that removal **cannot be undone**.

**Step 2.** Read the docket number in the message carefully. Compare it with your paper or source record.

**Expected result:** You are sure it is the correct docket.

**Step 3.** If you are certain, click **Remove permanently** (or equivalent). If you are not certain, click **Cancel**.

**Expected result:**  
- If you cancel, the dialog closes and nothing changes.  
- If you confirm and the action succeeds, a short success message appears and the list refreshes without that docket.

**Figure 19 — Remove docket confirmation dialog (place your screenshot here)**

**NOTE:** If you do not see **Remove**, your account is not permitted to delete dockets. Never ask another user to delete records on your behalf unless this follows an approved internal process.

## 7. Things that depend on your access rights

**Purpose:** PPIS hides or shows menu items and buttons based on your **role** and **permissions**.

You might find that:

- **Probation**, **Investigation**, **Update**, **Attachments**, or **Remove** do not appear at all.  
- You can **view** but not **update**.  
- You can **update** but not **remove**.

**Step 1.** If a button you need is missing, do **not** try to work around the system.

**Expected result:** You contact your **supervisor** or **system administrator** with the exact module name (**Probation — Investigation**) and what you need to do.

**Figure 20 — Example: Actions column with View only for one role (place your screenshot here)**

## 8. Quick reminders

1. **Use the official PPIS link** from your administrator.  
2. **Sign out** when finished, especially on shared PCs.  
3. Under **Probation**, choose **Investigation** for this module—not **Courtesy Investigation**.  
4. Use **Search** when the list is long; clear the search to return to the full office list.  
5. **View** is for reading; **Update** is for changing data.  
6. **Remove** is permanent—double-check before confirming.  
7. If the system says your **field office** cannot be determined, **sign in again** or call support.
